Answering that reproducibly meant fixing the tooling first. The thesis does both.

~3 min. Open with the question, not the tools. Context (Ch1, Ch5 intro): ~35% of men have biochemical recurrence after primary treatment; enzalutamide resistance is intrinsic in 20–40% and acquired in everyone eventually.

circRNAs are covalently closed, so they resist exonucleases (roughly 2.5x the half-life of linear RNA) and travel in exosomes: attractive biomarkers. But they are ~1% of the poly(A) pool and the tooling was unreliable. Walk the 40-minute plan along the bar: problem, tools, application, impact.

~4 min total for Act I (this slide ~2 min). Two failure modes. (1) Detection: only 854 of 5,071 circRNAs were found by all five tools; segmented vs pseudo-reference approaches and aligner choice drive the differences. Benchmarks (Zeng 2017, Gaffo 2022, Vromman 2023) agree: multi-tool consensus is the pragmatic answer.

(2) Annotation: the same circRNA has four unrelated identifiers across databases, built on different genome assemblies and 0-/1-based conventions. This sets up both tools in Act II.

~3 min of ~8 for this chapter. Three modules: discovery (7 quantification tools, BSJ and consensus filtering, annotation), miRNA prediction (miRanda + TargetScan, both must agree), differential expression (DESeq2, plus CircTest for circular-to-linear ratios).

Engineering story: built to nf-core standards (CI testing, pinned containers, peer review). Demo: re-analysed a published C. elegans dataset (PRJNA742881) in 18 h 21 m on an 8-CPU / 60 GB SLURM allocation; recovered 4,435 published circRNAs and found 2,420 more.

Act II · Build · Ch 3

Consensus beats every single tool

Rank by
Hover a bar for run time and memory. Simulated glioblastoma dataset, 7,267 circRNAs.
  • Six of seven tools are precise (≥97%); sensitivity ranges 63–92%.
  • Requiring ≥2 tools to agree gives 99.8% precision, 96.1% sensitivity.
  • Sweet spot: 3 tools, agreement of 2 (F1 97.8%); more tools add cost, not accuracy.
  • DCC (STAR 2-pass) adds false positives: 75.55% precision.

~3 min. Click through the three metrics: precision is uniformly high except DCC; sensitivity is where single tools differ and where consensus wins. Consensus here means a circRNA is kept if at least two tools call it (--tool_filter 2).

Combinatorial analysis over all tool subsets showed an F1 inflection at three tools with filter 2 (e.g. CIRCexplorer2 + CIRIquant + Segemehl: precision 98.5%, sensitivity 97.2%, F1 97.8%). Sets that include DCC score significantly worse. Practical guidance for users, from data.

Act III · Apply · Ch 5

Use regulatory biology, not brute force, to choose features

The ceRNA hypothesis predicts how three RNA types move together:

circRNA ↑⊣miRNA ↓⊣mRNA ↑
circRNA ↓⊣miRNA ↑⊣mRNA ↓

Only triplets whose expression moves as the hypothesis predicts survive, in both prostate tumours and enzalutamide-resistant LNCaP cells.

23 genesCoxBoost, then proportional-hazards check

~4 min of ~12 for this chapter. Click to step through the funnel (not to scale). The key idea: high-dimensional transcriptomics with few events overfits; the ceRNA network is a biologically motivated prior that shrinks 1,310 candidates to 817 before any survival modelling.

Data: 2 circRNA, 7 miRNA and 5 mRNA datasets, including in-house enzalutamide-resistant LNCaP clones (Clone 1 highly, Clone 9 moderately resistant). Modelling: CoxBoost with min/max steps chosen to keep 15–50 events per predictor; 26 non-zero coefficients, 3 removed for violating proportional hazards, leaving 23 genes.

~4 min. Lead with the honest number: apparent C-index 0.73 drops to 0.68 after optimism correction, and I report both. Then internal-external cross-validation across nine cohorts (n = 1,451): pooled calibration slope 1.61 with moderate heterogeneity (I² = 43%); discrimination heterogeneity not detectable.

External validation (GSE46602) is small: C = 0.71, 5-year AUC 0.87, but 36 patients so intervals are wide. Several published signatures score higher in that cohort (Long: 0.98) but only 2 of 23 genes overlap with any of them, which is the discovery argument. Expect viva questions on calibration and sample size.
